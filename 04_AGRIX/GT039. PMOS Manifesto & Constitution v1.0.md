# PMOS Manifesto & Constitution v1.0

## Preamble（前文）

> **Human civilization has achieved extraordinary progress.**
>
> We have connected billions of people through digital networks.
> We have decoded the human genome.
> We have explored the depths of the oceans and the surface of the Moon.
>
> Yet, despite these achievements, humanity continues to face a convergence of crises.
>
> Climate instability.
> Soil degradation.
> Food insecurity.
> Biodiversity loss.
> Chronic disease.
> Resource depletion.
>
> These challenges are commonly addressed as separate problems.
>
> **We believe they are not separate.**
>
> They are interconnected expressions of a disrupted planetary metabolism.

---

# Chapter 1

## A Different Question

The question is not,

> *How do we produce more food?*

Nor,

> *How do we reduce more carbon?*

Nor,

> *How do we lower healthcare costs?*

The more fundamental question is,

> **How does a living planet sustain itself?**

Only after understanding that question can lasting solutions emerge.

---

# Chapter 2

## Learning from Nature

Nature has operated successfully for billions of years.

Not through centralized control.

Not through isolated optimization.

But through continuous interaction among microorganisms, soil, water, plants, animals, and ecosystems.

These interactions create metabolic cycles that sustain life.

Human civilization has often attempted to improve upon nature.

PMOS begins from a different premise.

**The greatest innovations may come not from replacing nature, but from understanding how nature already works.**

---

# Chapter 3

## Planetary Metabolism

Planetary Metabolism is the continuous exchange of energy, nutrients, information, water, carbon, and biological activity that enables life on Earth.

When these metabolic relationships are weakened,

soil declines,

food systems deteriorate,

human health is affected,

ecosystems lose resilience,

and economic systems become increasingly expensive to sustain.

The restoration of these relationships creates multiple benefits simultaneously.

---

# Chapter 4

## The Principle of Stewardship

Humanity does not own planetary metabolism.

We participate in it.

Technology should therefore serve as a steward,

not as a substitute,

for nature's intelligence.

The purpose of innovation is not to dominate living systems,

but to help restore the conditions in which they can function naturally.

---

# Chapter 5

## PMOS

Planetary Metabolic Operating System (PMOS)

is proposed as an integrated knowledge architecture.

Its purpose is to connect

Ecological Intelligence,

Metabolic Intelligence,

Planetary Value Intelligence,

through a unified framework capable of supporting agriculture, health, climate, finance, and biosecurity.

PMOS is not a software product.

It is an architecture for understanding, organizing, and implementing planetary metabolism.

---

# Chapter 6

## A New Beginning

Every generation inherits challenges.

Some generations inherit opportunities.

Our generation inherits both.

The challenge is restoring the metabolism of a living planet.

The opportunity is to create the knowledge architecture that makes such restoration possible.

PMOS is proposed as one contribution toward that future.

Its success will not be measured by the technologies it creates,

but by the resilience of the living systems it helps restore.
