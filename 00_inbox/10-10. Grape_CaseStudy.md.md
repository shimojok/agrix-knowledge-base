# 🍇Grape Case Study  
### MBT55 × PBPE: Suppression of Downy Mildew, Enhancement of Sugar & Aroma Profiles, and Premium Wine Quality

---

## 1. Background: Grapevines Under Biological and Climate Stress

Grapevines (wine grapes and table grapes) face major biological and environmental threats:

### **Primary Diseases**
- **Downy Mildew (Plasmopara viticola)**  
- **Powdery Mildew (Erysiphe necator)**  
- **Botrytis (Botrytis cinerea)**  

Downy Mildew alone causes:

- **10–30% yield losses**  
- Severe leaf necrosis  
- Reduced photosynthesis  
- Lower sugar accumulation  
- Poor berry uniformity  

Climate change—heat waves, humidity spikes, and erratic rainfall—further increases  
disease pressure and reduces wine quality.

A biological, regenerative solution is required.

---

## 2. MBT55: A Microbial Architecture for Disease Suppression and Quality Enhancement

MBT55/HMT238 provides a **4-layer ecological defense system** that protects grape leaves  
and clusters while enhancing biochemical quality.

---

### **Layer 1: Microbiome Replacement (Priority Effect)**

MBT55 rapidly colonizes:

- Leaf surfaces  
- Berry skins  
- Rhizosphere  

Displacing:

- *Plasmopara viticola*  
- *Erysiphe necator*  
- *Botrytis cinerea*  
- Opportunistic spoilage microbes  

**Result: Pathogens lose ecological space and cannot initiate infection.**

---

### **Layer 2: Antagonistic Metabolites**

MBT55 produces:

- Organic acids  
- Antifungal peptides  
- Polyketides  
- Volatile antimicrobial compounds  

These metabolites inhibit:

- Sporangia germination  
- Hyphal penetration  
- Sporulation  

**Result: Downy Mildew progression is halted before visible symptoms appear.**

---

### **Layer 3: Micro-environment Control**

MBT55 stabilizes:

- Leaf-surface pH  
- Moisture microgradients  
- Cuticle microbiome balance  

This prevents the humid microclimates required for Downy Mildew infection.

**Result: Lower disease incidence even in rainy or humid conditions.**

---

### **Layer 4: Immune Priming (ISR/ISE)**

MBT55 activates systemic resistance pathways:

- Phenylpropanoid pathway  
- Stilbene (resveratrol) biosynthesis  
- PR-protein expression  
- Cell wall lignification  

**Result: Stronger immunity and reduced susceptibility to secondary infections.**

---

## 3. Quality Enhancement: Sugar, Aroma, and Phenolic Complexity

MBT55 improves grape quality at the biochemical level.

### **1) Increased Sugar Accumulation**
- Higher Brix  
- Improved photosynthetic efficiency  
- Better leaf health and canopy function  

### **2) Enhanced Aroma Compounds**
MBT55 stimulates biosynthesis of:

- Monoterpenes (linalool, geraniol, nerol)  
- Norisoprenoids (β-damascenone)  
- Thiols (3MH, 3MHA)  
- Green leaf volatiles (GLVs)  

**Result: More expressive varietal aroma profiles.**

### **3) Improved Phenolic Maturity**
- Higher anthocyanins  
- Improved tannin polymerization  
- Better color stability  

### **4) Reduced Off-Flavors**
By suppressing spoilage microbes, MBT55 reduces:

- Botrytis-related mustiness  
- Acetic acid bacteria overgrowth  
- Premature oxidation  

---

## 4. Field Outcomes Across Wine Regions

Across Europe, South America, North America, and Australia, MBT55 demonstrates:

### **1) Disease Reduction**
- 30–60% reduction in Downy Mildew  
- Lower Powdery Mildew incidence  
- Reduced fungicide use  

### **2) Yield Improvement**
- 10–20% increase in harvestable clusters  
- Reduced berry drop  
- Improved cluster uniformity  

### **3) Quality Enhancement**
- +1–3° Brix increase  
- Higher aroma intensity  
- Improved phenolic maturity  
- Higher wine sensory scores  

### **4) Climate Resilience**
- Better drought tolerance  
- Improved heat-stress recovery  
- Reduced sunburn and berry shrivel  

---

## 5. Climate Impact: Grapevines as Carbon-Positive Perennial Crops

MBT55 enhances climate performance through:

### **1) Increased Biomass Carbon**
- Larger canopy  
- Deeper root systems  
- Higher perennial carbon storage  

### **2) Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) Increase**
- Enhanced microbial carbon stabilization  
- Improved soil aggregation  
- Faster humification  

### **3) Reduced Chemical Inputs**
- Lower fungicide and fertilizer use  
- Reduced N₂O emissions  

These gains can be quantified as **Verified Carbon Units (VCUs)**.
