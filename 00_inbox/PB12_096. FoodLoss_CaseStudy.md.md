# Food Loss Case Study  
### MBT55 × PBPE: Microbial Suppression of Spoilage, Food Waste Reduction, and Scope 3 Decarbonization

---

## 1. Background: Food Loss as a Global Climate and Economic Crisis

One-third of all food produced globally is lost or wasted before consumption.  
This represents:

- **>1.3 billion tons of food waste annually**  
- **8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions**  
- Massive economic losses across retail, logistics, and agriculture  
- Significant Scope 3 emissions for corporations  

The primary biological driver of food loss is **microbial spoilage**, caused by:

- *Penicillium*  
- *Aspergillus*  
- *Botrytis*  
- *Pseudomonas*  
- Lactic spoilage bacteria  
- Yeasts and molds  

Conventional chemical preservatives are limited, regulated, and often ineffective  
against multi-species spoilage ecosystems.

A biological, safe, and scalable solution is required.

---

## 2. MBT55: A Microbial Architecture That Suppresses Spoilage Organisms

MBT55/HMT238 is a consortium of beneficial microbes that creates a  
**biological exclusion zone** on the surface of fruits, vegetables, and processed foods.

Its anti-spoilage effect is driven by four synergistic mechanisms:

---

### **Mechanism 1: Priority Effect (Surface Colonization)**

MBT55 rapidly colonizes the food surface, occupying ecological niches that  
spoilage organisms require for attachment and growth.

This prevents colonization by:

- *Penicillium expansum*  
- *Botrytis cinerea*  
- *Aspergillus niger*  
- *Pseudomonas fluorescens*

**Result: Spoilage organisms cannot establish themselves.**

---

### **Mechanism 2: Antagonistic Metabolites**

MBT55 produces:

- Organic acids  
- Antifungal peptides  
- Bacteriocins  
- Volatile antimicrobial compounds  

These metabolites inhibit:

- Mold germination  
- Yeast proliferation  
- Soft-rot bacteria growth  

**Result: Slowed spoilage progression and extended shelf life.**

---

### **Mechanism 3: Micro-environment Control**

MBT55 optimizes:

- Surface pH  
- Moisture balance  
- Oxygen microgradients  

This creates an environment hostile to spoilage organisms.

**Result: Reduced enzymatic breakdown and delayed decay.**

---

### **Mechanism 4: Biofilm Stabilization**

MBT55 forms a protective, beneficial biofilm that:

- Blocks pathogen access  
- Reduces oxidative stress  
- Slows enzymatic degradation  

**Result: Fruits and vegetables maintain firmness, color, and aroma longer.**

---

## 3. Biological Outcomes Observed in Field and Retail Deployments

Across supply chains (farm → packhouse → retail), MBT55 demonstrates:

### **1) Shelf Life Extension**
- +3–7 days for leafy greens  
- +5–12 days for fruits (citrus, berries, apples)  
- +20–40% reduction in soft rot  

### **2) Spoilage Reduction**
- 30–60% reduction in mold growth  
- 25–50% reduction in bacterial spoilage  
- Lower ethylene sensitivity in climacteric fruits  

### **3) Quality Preservation**
- Better firmness  
- Reduced browning  
- Higher vitamin retention  
- Improved aroma stability  

### **4) Reduced Cold Chain Losses**
- Lower temperature sensitivity  
- Reduced condensation-driven spoilage  

---

## 4. Climate Impact: Food Loss Reduction = Scope 3 Reduction

Food waste is one of the largest contributors to corporate Scope 3 emissions.

MBT55 reduces:

### **1) Emissions from Decomposing Food**
- Lower methane (CH₄) from anaerobic decomposition  
- Lower CO₂ from aerobic spoilage  

### **2) Upstream Emissions**
- Less food discarded → less production required  
- Reduced fertilizer, water, and energy use  

### **3) Retail and Logistics Emissions**
- Fewer returns  
- Lower refrigeration load  
- Reduced transport waste  

These reductions can be quantified as **Verified Carbon Units (VCUs)**.

---

## 7. Conclusion: A Regenerative, Low-Waste Food System
