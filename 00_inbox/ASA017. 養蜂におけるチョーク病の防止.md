We have field documentation — not a controlled trial, I want to be precise about that — of a multi-species microbial consortium eliminating chalkbrood (Ascosphaera apis) in treated hives, where untreated hives nearby continued to show infection. What's puzzling is the shape of it: single-agent biocontrol usually gives you a dose-dependent, gradual reduction in infection. What we recorded looks more like a threshold — infection present, then essentially absent, without much of a gradient in between. My working hypothesis is that niche pre-emption and antibiotic secretion by different members of the consortium are acting on the pathogen's effective R0 at the same time, and R0 crossing 1 produces exactly that kind of non-linear switch rather than a smooth decline. I know the theory; I don't have the epidemiological rigor to know if I'm reading the field data correctly.
