# Methane Reduction Credit Model (MRC)

The Methane Reduction Credit (MRC) quantifies reductions in CH₄ emissions  
achieved through MBT55-driven microbial optimization, fermentation shifts,  
and methanotroph activation.  
MRC is a core climate credit within the PBPE system.

---

# 1. Overview

Methane (CH₄) is produced in:

- Ruminant digestion
- Anaerobic soils
- Rice paddies
- Manure and slurry
- Organic waste piles

CH₄ has **84×** the warming potential of CO₂ (20-year GWP),  
making its reduction a high-value climate asset.

---

# 2. MBT55 Methane Reduction Mechanisms

## 2.1 Fermentation Shift Toward SCFA
MBT55 increases:

- Acetate
- Propionate
- Butyrate

This reduces hydrogen availability for methanogenesis.

---

## 2.2 Methanotroph Activation
MBT55 stimulates:

- Type I methanotrophs
- Type II methanotrophs

These microbes **consume methane** as an energy source.

---

## 2.3 Redox Cycling
Mn/Fe cycling:

- Competes with methanogenesis
- Provides alternative electron sinks
- Reduces anaerobic hotspots

---

## 2.4 Aerobic Microzone Formation
MBT55 increases:

- Aerobic bacteria
- Oxygen diffusion
- Surface oxidation

This suppresses methane-producing archaea.

---

# 3. MRC Calculation

\[
MRC = (CH4_{baseline} - CH4_{after}) \times A \times MF
\]

Where:

- \(CH4_{baseline}\) = methane emissions before MBT55  
- \(CH4_{after}\) = methane emissions after MBT55  
- \(A\) = system scale (animals, hectares, ponds)  
- \(MF\) = methane financial factor  

---

# 4. Verification Methods

- Gas flux chambers  
- Laser CH₄ sensors  
- Manure emission analysis  
- Rice paddy gas sampling  
- Livestock respiration chambers  

---

# 5. Financial Integration

MRC feeds into:

- Carbon markets  
- ESG climate portfolios  
- PBPE-Finance  
- PBPE-Dashboard  

---

# 6. One-Sentence Summary

**MRC quantifies methane reductions achieved through MBT55-driven fermentation  
optimization, methanotroph activation, and redox cycling.**

# Nitrous Oxide Reduction Credit Model (NRC)

The Nitrous Oxide Reduction Credit (NRC) quantifies reductions in N₂O emissions  
achieved through MBT55-driven nitrogen stabilization, microbial nitrogen retention,  
and suppression of nitrification–denitrification spikes.  
NRC is one of the highest-value climate credits in PBPE.

---

# 1. Overview

N₂O emissions originate from:

- Nitrification
- Denitrification
- Excess fertilizer
- Anaerobic soil pockets
- Microbial imbalance

N₂O has **298×** the warming potential of CO₂,  
making its reduction extremely valuable.

---

# 2. MBT55 N₂O Reduction Mechanisms

## 2.1 Organic Acid Regulation
Organic acids:

- Slow nitrification
- Reduce nitrate accumulation
- Stabilize soil pH microzones

---

## 2.2 Microbial Nitrogen Retention
MBT55 increases:

- Microbial biomass nitrogen (MBN)
- Amino-N retention
- Organic-N formation

This keeps nitrogen **in the soil**, not in the atmosphere.

---

## 2.3 Redox Cycling
MBT55 stabilizes nitrate by:

- Mn/Fe cycling
- Electron shuttling
- Reducing denitrification spikes

---

## 2.4 Soil Structure Improvement
Better aggregation reduces:

- Anaerobic pockets
- N₂O hotspots

---

# 3. NRC Calculation

\[
NRC = (N2O_{baseline} - N2O_{after}) \times A \times NF
\]

Where:

- \(N2O_{baseline}\) = emissions before MBT55  
- \(N2O_{after}\) = emissions after MBT55  
- \(A\) = land area  
- \(NF\) = nitrogen financial factor  

---

# 4. Verification Methods

- Gas flux chambers  
- Soil nitrate analysis  
- Fertilizer efficiency data  
- Remote sensing  
- Soil redox measurements  

---

# 5. Financial Integration

NRC feeds into:

- Carbon markets  
- Nitrogen efficiency programs  
- PBPE-Finance  
- PBPE-Dashboard

# 6. One-Sentence Summary

**NRC quantifies MBT55-driven reductions in N₂O emissions through nitrogen
